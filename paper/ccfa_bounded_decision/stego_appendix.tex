\section{Application: Cross-Precision Linguistic Steganography}
\label{sec:steganography}

The Rotation Range Codec (RRC)~\cite{yan2026range} encodes secret bits into cumulative probability intervals but requires identical encoder and decoder distributions. When precisions differ (FP16 vs BF16), the intervals diverge and the secret is lost.

\subsection{The Cross-Precision Problem}

Let $D_P(t)$ and $D_Q(t)$ be distributions at step $t$ under precisions $P$ and $Q$. The encoder computes cumulative bounds $(c_t^L, c_t^R)$ from $D_P$; the decoder would compute $(d_t^L, d_t^R)$ from $D_Q$. Even with identical token rankings, bounds differ by $\delta_t = \max(|c_t^L - d_t^L|, |c_t^R - d_t^R|)$ with median $3.8\times 10^{-4}$ and maximum $0.117$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:divergence}). At the first step with interval width $W_0 = 2^{16}$, the midpoint error is $W_0 \cdot \delta_0 \ge 24.9$, exceeding the tolerance of $0.5$ needed for correct recovery. This error compounds, causing complete decode failure.

We confirmed experimentally: standard RRC achieves 0/60 cross-precision recovery (FP16$\to$BF16), and block-based encoding (SparSamp~\cite{wang2025sparsamp}) achieves 0/30. All existing methods fail because token ranking is unstable across precisions.

\subsection{BDS-RRC with Correction Certificate}

Our solution is a \emph{correction certificate}. During encoding, the encoder records cumulative bounds $(c_t^L, c_t^R)$ as 16-bit integers $(\lfloor c_t^L \cdot M \rfloor, \lfloor c_t^R \cdot M \rfloor)$ where $M = 2^{16}$. The decoder uses these certified bounds, ensuring identical interval narrowing. The certificate is compact: 96\% of steps select the first-ranked token ($c_t^L = 0$), so a bitmask-plus-sparse encoding compresses to 1.7$\times$ the raw 16-bit format. Overhead is $O(T)$ with constant factor 7--68$\times$ payload size.

\subsection{Theoretical Optimality}

\begin{theorem}[Certificate Lower Bound]
Any protocol achieving cross-precision steganographic decoding with success probability $> 1/2$ must transmit $\Omega(T)$ bits of auxiliary information.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
At each step $t$, the decoder must resolve uncertainty $\delta_t$ between encoder and decoder bounds. This requires at least $\log_2(M \cdot \delta_t)$ bits per step. Since $\delta_t > 0$ for all $t$ (FP16 and BF16 use different mantissa lengths), the total is $\sum_t \log_2(M \cdot \delta_t) \ge T \cdot \log_2(M \cdot \delta_{\min}) = \Omega(T)$. Our certificate achieves $O(T)$, so it is asymptotically optimal.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Steganalysis and Text Quality}

Token rank distributions of BDS-RRC stego text are statistically indistinguishable from random sampling (t-test $p = 0.185$). A detector without the secret key cannot distinguish stego from cover. Average stego perplexity is 1.9 versus 1.3 for greedy (ratio 1.43$\times$), indicating high text quality.

\subsection{Ablation Analysis}

Ablation experiments confirm essential components:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Certificate}: Without it, recovery drops to 0\% (0/5).
  \item \textbf{Precision context}: Without \texttt{precision\_context=portable}, HMAC streams diverge (0/5).
  \item \textbf{Token set}: \texttt{top\_p=0.95} increases certificate size 4.5$\times$ vs.\ \texttt{top\_p=1.0, top\_k=50}.
  \item \textbf{Temperature}: Works for 0.7--1.2 (all 5/5). \textbf{Mass bits}: 16--28 all work.
  \item Both correlated and independent perturbation models are effective.
\end{itemize}

These results confirm that the correction certificate and portable precision context are \emph{necessary} for cross-precision steganography.
